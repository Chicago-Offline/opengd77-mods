gd77-mods KC9MHE · WRXC682

The quieter status bar

OpenGD77 · MD-UV380 / RT3S

The stock header puts six things in one row of eight pixels. I kept two of them and gave the rest of the space to a meter.

Count what the top of the screen is carrying: the mode (FM, FMN or DMR), the CTCSS or DCS tone, the transmit power, the timeslot, the colour code, and the battery. Six pieces of information, one eight-pixel strip, all of it set in the smallest font the radio owns.

Every one of those is worth knowing. Almost none of it is worth knowing right now, mid-conversation, at arm's length. When I glance at a handheld I'm asking two questions — am I hearing this person well, and is the battery going to last — and the stock header answers neither of them quickly, because the answers are the same size as everything else.

What it looks like instead

The mod reduces the header to two rows: 

[S-meter reading]                      [battery]
[===== S-meter bar with S-unit marks =====]

Signal on the left, battery on the right, and underneath them a segmented bar that uses the width the other five items were fighting over. The bar is six pixels tall rather than the stock four, with the segments stepping up in height from left to right — a weak signal is short as well as narrow, so you can read it from the shape without reading the number.

Transmit folds into the same space. The reading on the left becomes your power level, and the bar carries on as the stock mic-level meter. If you'd rather watch power than your own voice, there's a setting for that too, and the bar becomes a power bar while you're keyed up.

The glyph on the far left

There's a small status glyph at the left end of the meter, and it's colour coded: cyan for an analog carrier, amber when a CTCSS or DCS tone is decoding, green for DMR, red while transmitting. That's the mode indicator the header used to spell out, except now it's a colour you catch in peripheral vision instead of three letters you have to focus on.

Those colours are hardcoded, which deserves an explanation, because hardcoding colour in a themeable UI is normally the wrong answer.

The honest reason is that deferring to the theme doesn't work here. In themeInitToDefaultValues(), the stock theme assigns the same foreground colour to FG_RSSI_BAR, FG_RSSI_BAR_S9P, FG_CHANNEL_CONTACT, FG_CSS_SQL_VALUES and FG_TX_FREQ. Drive the glyph from theme items and all four states come out identical on a stock radio — a colour code that codes nothing. Same problem for the S9+ segments: they're the only scale reference left once the graduations are off, and they're the same colour as the rest of the bar, so the boundary would be invisible. So the glyph palette and the S9+ marker are fixed, and the body of the bar still follows your theme. If you'd rather have the theme back, one #define hands it over.

It's a setting

This started as compile-time defines, which is fine when you're the only person building it and useless to anybody else. It's a menu item now: Status hdr, in display options, with three values. 

1   stock header
2   S-meter and battery
3   S-meter and battery, power bar on transmit

It ships on 1. A radio you flash and don't touch looks exactly like a stock one, and nothing about this is load-bearing — if you don't like it, put it back to 1 and the old header returns.

The two bits behind it were spare. OpenGD77 carries a block of unused option bits in non-volatile settings; this takes two of them, so the setting survives a power cycle without growing the settings structure or disturbing anything already stored in it.

Get the patch

chioff-quiet-status.patch — a plain unified diff against the OpenGD77 R20260131 release source. Three files, nothing binary in it. 

git apply --3way chioff-quiet-status.patch

Run it from the root of your OpenGD77 checkout. It touches uiUtilities.c, menuDisplayOptions.c and settings.h, and nothing outside the display path.

Or just flash the build

chioff-quiet-status-R20260131.bin — OpenGD77 R20260131 with this patch and nothing else. 806,800 bytes, for MD-UV380 / RT3S. 

sha256  b06cdc6bb96760cef6234e4f1952a74ceee1558d9d54f7a09280edd835e23714
md5     9f12204686ff8765aa2059db109d244a

Flash it with grid.radio/opengd77 — Custom File tab, pick the .bin, leave the codec donor on Built-in. There's no AMBE vocoder in this image; the OpenGD77 sources don't contain one, so that region of flash is left empty and your flashing tool merges a vocoder in from its own donor at write time.
